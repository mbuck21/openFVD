## E2: a valley against a level (entry speed 30 m/s)

Marks are metres of centre line from the start of the track; the first 50 m are the Transport section and the valley starts after about 120 m of level. In the program's own model the loss is friction (the same on all three tracks: it is charged per metre of track) plus air drag, and the drag is larger in a valley because the train is faster there. So the "loss" column is NOT the same on the three tracks: it differs by 0.25 m^2/s^2 (1.5 g valley) and 1.04 m^2/s^2 (4 g valley) from the level; the "drag part" column differs by 0.23 and 1.03 (the small rest is the rounding of the node steps), with the program's drag of 2e-05. **Scale those two numbers by the drag you fitted in E1 divided by 2e-05 before reading an excess.** An excess over the scaled number means friction grows with wheel load. The last column shows how big that would be: friction that follows the vertical force (g x friction x (valley g - 1) x metres held at the valley g, with the program's friction 0.03) would add the number in "extra if friction follows load". It is much larger than the drag differences, so the two effects can be told apart.
